# Spinola

Native SwiftUI iOS trivia game for couples (iOS 17+, iPhone and iPad). Two "worlds": His World and Her World. Two modes: **couple** (you vs your partner: each declares the world they know and is quizzed on the other's) and **teams** (your couple vs another couple: each member has a lane, the world they answer, and the other couple picks a deck per member; scores add up). Five rounds, seven questions each, difficulty escalates from Rookie to Legend, first to three crowns wins. Async on two phones through Game Center turn-based matches (one phone per side, a couple shares theirs), or pass-and-play on one phone.

## Non-negotiables
- **Opinionated, not generic.** The his/her framing is the identity. Ola (the host) has a voice: dry, warm, never scolding.
- **Facts must be right.** Every question is a verifiable public fact. No opinions dressed as facts. Real people, brands, and titles are fine in trivia; keep them factual and non-defamatory.
- **App Store safe.** No accounts of our own, no passwords, no server, no third-party SDKs. Game Center only (the `com.apple.developer.game-center` entitlement). Keep `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` accurate.
- **Design system (game-show register, like Trivia Crack but ours)**: solid world-colored backgrounds with slow sunburst rays, a halftone dot band and paper grain (`GameBackground`), cream chunky panels with a bottom edge (`.panel()`), 3D `ChunkyButtonStyle` buttons, a `Mascot` per deck with moods, a spinning `WheelView` for picks, `TimerRing`, `ConfettiBurst`. Type: Rockwell (slab serif) for anything loud or read, DIN Condensed for scoreboard numbers and kickers; both ship with iOS, nothing bundled. Icons: every glyph is drawn from paths in `Views/Icons.swift` (`DeckIcon`, `CrownIcon`, `TrophyIcon`, `HandoffIcon`, `Glyph`); no SF Symbols anywhere in the UI. Canvas quirk: a closed path of two quad curves won't fill, use cubics. `IconSheetTests` renders a contact sheet of all icons to the test host's tmp dir for review. Never revert to a flat text/paper interface or to gradients + SF Symbols.
- **One layout, two devices.** Universal build (iPhone portrait, iPad all orientations). Every screen is a `Stage`: background bleeds to all four edges, content sits in a centered play column with the phone's proportions. `StageScroll` is the scrolling variant and centers content shorter than the screen. A screen with a header on top and buttons at the bottom is a `PinnedColumn`: the middle sits centred between them and scrolls when it doesn't fit (iPhone SE, xxLarge text), so the buttons never leave the screen; don't go back to a `VStack` with `Spacer`s, which pushed them off the SE. `Viewport` measures the window (not the device, because iPadOS 26 makes every window resizable) and reports `UI.scale` against the 393x852 phone design; the `Font` helpers, `ChunkyButtonStyle`, `.panel()` and the drawn art all multiply by it, so one dial grows everything together. Two traps: never nest a `Stage` inside another (it clips the inner background to the outer column and reads as a seam down both sides of an iPad), and wrap any hand-placed padding that has to clear scaled art in `UI.s()` or the art lands on the text. Fonts inside fixed-frame art take final points via `.logo()`/`.artScore()`, which do not scale again.
- **All ages.** The audience runs from a 30-year-old wife to a 65-year-old father playing at family game night. Reading text never goes below 14pt (the `Font` helpers enforce floors), fonts scale with Dynamic Type (capped at xxLarge in `RootView`), secondary text uses `ink2` not `ink3`, tap targets are 44pt, and every looping animation and the confetti respect Reduce Motion. Keep it fun, not childish: game-show, not cartoon. Color is loud on small things (wheel, tiles, mascots, wordmark) and calm on big ones: any full-screen color goes through `GameBackground`, which pulls it toward night. Noah's words: "the colors are a bit much" when a screen was solid saturated color.

## Architecture
- **Android exists** (`android/`, Kotlin + Jetpack Compose; see the Android section below). Keep rules and match state in plain Swift with no UI imports and keep `MatchState` JSON stable: it is the cross-platform contract, and the Kotlin tests replay fixtures the Swift tests write.
- `Models/`: `Deck`/`Question` (content), `MatchState` (the JSON both phones agree on: `MatchPlayer` sides with `Member`s, picks and results keyed by member id), `Profile`.
- `Content/HerWorld.swift`, `Content/HisWorld.swift` (+ `*More.swift`): 18 decks × 120 questions (40 per tier): the original 36 inline, then 84 appended from `Content/Extra/<Deck>Extra.swift`. Question ids are positional (`<deck>-<n>`), so only ever append; inserting or deleting renumbers every later question and breaks matches in flight. Author with `Q(tier, prompt, correct, [wrong×3], fact?)`. Option order is shuffled deterministically per question id.
- `Engine/MatchEngine.swift`: pure rules (question draw, tiers per round, scoring). `Engine/MatchController.swift`: one match's UI state machine (`route()` is the single source of what's next; hand-offs between members of a couple on one phone); it talks to a `MatchTransport`. Never leave an async submit in flight without first setting a stage the views can render.
- `Services/GameCenterService.swift` (+ `GameCenterTransport`), `Services/LocalMatchStore.swift` (+ `PassAndPlayTransport`).
- `Views/`: `HomeView` (match lists), `MatchView` (stage switch), `DeckPickView`, `QuestionView`, `RoundRevealView`, `MatchOverView`, `ProfileSetupView`.
- `Audio/SoundKit.swift`: synthesized cues, no audio files.

## Workflow
- Project is generated: edit `project.yml`, run `xcodegen generate`. Never hand-edit the `.xcodeproj`.
- Build/test: `xcodebuild test -project OlaBall.xcodeproj -scheme OlaBall -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,id=22F189D4-EAEA-44E5-A62A-DFF364B638E5'` (iPhone 16 Pro). The UI test plays a full pass-and-play match and writes screenshots to `build/shots` (override with `TEST_RUNNER_OLABALL_SHOTS=<dir>`); run it against an iPad destination to check that layout, and against the iPhone SE (3rd generation) for the shortest phone. `WaitingScreenTests` renders the waiting screen, which only online play reaches, to the same folder. App Store screenshots: `./scripts/store_shots.sh` and `./scripts/store_shots.sh ipad`. Submission checklist: `docs/APP-STORE.md`.
- Game Center needs a signed-in sandbox account on the simulator (Settings > Game Center) to test online; pass-and-play covers everything else.
- The football prototype lives at tag `v1-football-draw-the-play`.

## Android
- `android/` is a native Kotlin + Jetpack Compose port, pass-and-play only (no Game Center equivalent on Android). Two Gradle modules: `core` (pure Kotlin: `support/SeededRNG`, `model/`, `engine/`, `services/`; JVM-tested) and `app` (Compose: `ui/theme`, `ui/hud`, `ui/screens`, `audio`). Same package prefix `co.nsgsolutions.spinola`, same file-per-screen structure as `OlaBall/Views`, same strings.
- **Content and parity are generated, not copied.** `OlaBallTests/CrossPlatformExportTests.swift` writes `content/assets/decks.json` (the Android asset) and `content/golden/*.json`. After editing questions or rules: run that Swift test (it rewrites the files and fails once), commit the files, then `cd android && ./gradlew :core:test`. Never hand-edit `content/`.
- The same design rules apply: `AppText` for every text style (floors and canvas scale inside), `UI.s()` for hand-placed sizes, `Theme` colours, icons drawn in `ui/hud/Icons.kt`, no `androidx.compose.material.icons`, no gradient backgrounds, `LocalReduceMotion` on every loop. Rockwell and DIN Condensed Bold are stood in by bundled Arvo and Barlow Condensed Bold (SIL OFL, in `res/font`, licences in `assets/licenses`), named only in `Fonts` in `ui/theme/Theme.kt`.
- Screens shaped like header / Spacer / middle / Spacer / buttons use `PinnedColumn` (`ui/hud/Hud.kt`), never raw weighted Spacers: a 16:9 phone crushed the question screen's Next button. Store screenshots come from a 9:16 emulator (`-skin 1080x1920`): Play rejects anything more than twice as tall as it is wide.
- Build: `export JAVA_HOME=/opt/homebrew/opt/openjdk@17/libexec/openjdk.jdk/Contents/Home ANDROID_HOME=$HOME/Library/Android/sdk; cd android; ./gradlew :core:test :app:testDebugUnitTest :app:assembleDebug`. Release: `./gradlew :app:bundleRelease`, signed with the upload key in `android/spinola-upload.jks` + `android/keystore.properties` (gitignored, on this Mac only; never regenerate them, Play pins the upload key). Play-through with screenshots on the `spinola_phone` emulator: `./scripts/store_shots.sh`. Stop Gradle daemons (`./gradlew --stop`) and iOS simulators before booting the emulator; this Mac runs out of disk through swap. Submission: `docs/PLAY-STORE.md`.
- **The match controller never lives in the composition.** `ui/screens/MatchHost.kt` (an activity-scoped ViewModel) holds the one open `MatchController`, so a tablet rotation, fold, font-size or dark-mode change (all of which recreate the activity) keeps the round in progress; iOS gets this free from `@State`. `RootView` releases it when the cover closes. Screen-level UI state that iOS keeps in `@State` goes in `rememberSaveable` for the same reason.
- Anything that shows time (the timer ring) reads the wall clock, never a Compose animation: "Remove animations" sets the animator scale to 0 and animations jump to their end. Open-ended frame loops use `androidx.compose.animation.core.withInfiniteAnimationFrameNanos` (the `ui.platform` one is internal); a plain `withFrameNanos` loop blocks every `waitForIdle()` in instrumented tests.
- Back is ignored mid-question (iOS has no exit there); everywhere else it closes like the close button.
